# The bio portrait

The portrait in the bio section is a cutout of the figure over a CSS ground.
Signe picks the photograph and the ground in the CMS; the build does the rest.

## What the editor controls

Two fields in the Bio collection, both shared across locales:

- `portrait`: the photograph, from the media library.
- `portraitGround`: `white`, `warm`, `chartreuse` or `cool`.

The grounds are CSS in `src/lib/components/Portrait.svelte` and are light on
purpose: a dark jacket needs something to stand against. Adding one means a
new `data-ground` rule there, a new entry in `GROUNDS`, and a new option
under `portraitGround` in `static/admin/config.yml`.

## What the build does

`scripts/gen-portrait.ts` runs in `prebuild`:

1. **Master.** A source under 900 px wide is upscaled first. ESRGAN (UpscalerJS
   on `@tensorflow/tfjs-node`) where that binding loads, which is linux/amd64;
   a Lanczos resize elsewhere. A Lanczos master is redone the first time the
   script runs somewhere ESRGAN is available.
2. **Cutout.** `scripts/matte.ts`, described below.
3. **Encode.** AVIF and WebP at 480 to 1600 px into `static/gen/portrait/`,
   named by content hash, and an entry in `src/lib/portrait-manifest.json`.

The step is gated on a hash of the photograph and the three scripts. The
generated files and the manifest are committed, so an ordinary build does no
image work. When Signe replaces the portrait in the CMS, the staging build
that her save triggers does the work inside the image build. It adds a few
minutes to that one deploy.

`static/gen/` sits outside the CMS media folder (`static/img/`), so generated
files never show up in the media library.

## How the cutout is made

A portrait against a wall is not a studio plate. The wall carries the figure's
cast shadow, and it shows through between an arm and the body. A colour keyer
reads both as figure, because both differ from the backing. A matting network
knows neither is a person, but its edges are soft at the resolution it runs at.
`matte.ts` uses each for what it measures:

- **BEN2** (MIT licence, ONNX, run locally through `@huggingface/transformers`)
  says which pixels are the person.
- **`keyer.ts`**, the known-backing keyer shared with gosscounselling.co.uk,
  solves exact coverage and the figure's own colour through the soft edge
  (Smith and Blinn, 1996).

They meet in a **clean plate**: the wall as it would look with the person
taken away, shadow included. Where the network is sure there is only wall,
the plate is the photograph. Around and behind the figure the plate is that
shadow carried inward. The figure is then solved against the plate, and the
plate's darkening is kept as a second, translucent layer under the figure. One
image carries both. Over white it reproduces the photograph; over any other
ground the shadow darkens and tints that ground the way it did the wall.

Where the wall is evenly lit, the keyer's result is used untouched.

## When a photograph does not cooperate

Nothing here fails a build on an editor's upload.

- **Backing too uneven to key** (a wall lit from one side, a room): the cutout
  is made from the network's matte alone, with foreground colour estimated by
  blur fusion (Forte and Pitié, ICIP 2021). No shadow layer.
- **Network unavailable** (the weights cannot be fetched): the manifest
  records why, and the page shows the photograph as it is, in the same frame.

The manifest entry says which route a portrait took (`cutout.method`, or
`uncut` with the reason).

## Running it by hand

```sh
pkgx pnpm portrait             # key the current portrait, if anything changed
pkgx pnpm portrait --force     # regardless of the gate
pkgx pnpm portrait --source some.jpg --out-dir /tmp/audit
                               # any photograph, into its own folder, drawn
                               # over light, mid and dark test grounds
```

The network's weights (219 MB, pinned by revision in `matte.ts`) download once
into `node_modules/.cache/matte-models`. Measured on 2 vCPU: 24 s and 2.9 GB
peak for the matte, about 80 s for the ESRGAN upscale of a 560 px source. A
standard GitHub-hosted runner has more of both.

## Open-graph images

The link-preview template (`/og/...`) draws the same component, so the OG
images follow the portrait and its ground. They are regenerated by hand on a
machine with a GPU (`pkgx pnpm og`); `og-drift-check.yml` opens an issue when
they fall behind.
